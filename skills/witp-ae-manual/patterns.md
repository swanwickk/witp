# War in the Pacific: Admiral's Edition (WitP-AE) - Master Patterns & SOPs

This document synthesizes core operational procedures, strategic algorithms, and standard operating procedures (SOPs) detailed in the official WitP-AE manual.

---

## 1. Naval Operations Patterns

### Pattern: Task Force Organization & Doctrine Balancing
- **When to use**: Whenever forming any naval task force (Surface Combat, Carrier Strike, Amphibious Assault, Fast Transport, ASW).
- **How**:
  1. *Speed Homogeneity*: Group ships with similar maximum and cruising speeds. A 12-knot auxiliary in an 18-knot transport group limits the entire TF to 12 knots.
  2. *Escort Ratio*: Maintain a minimum ratio of 1.5 to 2 destroyers/escorts per capital ship or major transport.
  3. *Commander Selection*: For Surface Combat TFs, assign commanders with high Naval (SEA) and Aggressiveness ratings; for Amphibious/Transport TFs, assign commanders with high Leadership/Admin ratings.
  4. *Patrol & Fuel Management*: Set waypoint routing to avoid high-threat enemy air umbrellas; set TF retreat thresholds to prevent ships outrunning their fuel reserves.
- **Trade-offs**: Heavily escorted TFs disperse scarce escort hulls across secondary routes; fast TFs burn fuel exponentially faster than cruise-speed groups.

### Pattern: Fast Transport / Tokyo Express Run
- **When to use**: Delivering urgent ground reinforcements, engineers, or supplies to contested or besieged front-line bases lacking functional port infrastructure (Port Level 0–1).
- **How**:
  1. Form a Fast Transport TF using high-speed Destroyers (DD) or High-Speed Transports (APD).
  2. Load ground troops or supply at a major staging base with ample supplies and high port level.
  3. Plot destination to the target contested hex; ensure arrival coincides with night phase.
  4. Order automatic retirement to a safe base out of daytime dive-bomber range before dawn.
- **Trade-offs**: Fast transports carry minimal heavy equipment and vehicles (squads land light); high fuel expenditure.

### Pattern: Submarine Wolfpack Interdiction & Choke-point Blockade
- **When to use**: Severing Japanese crude oil / resource lifelines from the Netherlands East Indies, or Allied supply convoys to Australia/Hawaii.
- **How**:
  1. Identify historical choke-points: Luzon Strait, Makassar Strait, Sunda Strait, Malacca Strait, and approaches to Truk/Tokyo.
  2. Station fleet submarines on 1-hex patrol zones with standing orders to submerge during daylight and surface for battery recharge during night.
  3. Form overlapping coverage screens spaced 80–120 nautical miles (2–3 hexes) apart rather than stacking submarines in a single hex (avoiding detection spillover).
- **Trade-offs**: High submarine density in narrow straits increases susceptibility to enemy land-based ASW aircraft patrol.

## 2. Air Warfare Patterns

### Pattern: The Layered Defensive Air Umbrella (CAP Doctrine)
- **When to use**: Defending vital ports, capital carrier formations, or forward airfields against massed enemy torpedo/level bomber strikes.
- **How**:
  1. *Altitude Stacking*: Segment defending fighter squadrons into distinct altitude bands:
     - High Band (25,000–30,000 ft): Dedicated sweep interceptors gaining energy/dive advantage over enemy escorts.
     - Medium Band (15,000–20,000 ft): Main CAP body engaging enemy escort fighters and high-level bombers.
     - Low Band (5,000–10,000 ft): Anti-torpedo bomber screen engaging low-altitude naval strike packages.
  2. *Readiness Rotation*: Set CAP allocation to 60–70% maximum; retain 30–40% on Rest/Reserve to cycle tired pilots and maintain constant readiness over multiple combat pulses.
  3. *Radar & Visual Warning*: Emplace radar-equipped base forces or radar pickets (destroyers) 1–2 hexes forward in threat vectors.
- **Trade-offs**: Excessive CAP commitment induces severe pilot fatigue; splitting altitude reduces local numerical superiority at any single altitude.

### Pattern: Fighter Sweep Pre-Emptive Suppression
- **When to use**: The turn before launching massed naval or ground strike bombers against a heavily defended enemy airbase.
- **How**:
  1. Designate high-experience fighter squadrons on "Fighter Sweep" mission.
  2. Set sweep altitude 2,000–3,000 feet higher than enemy standard CAP operating ceilings.
  3. Time strike bomber packages to launch in follow-up pulses, ensuring defending CAP is depleted of ammunition, scattered, and fatigued.
- **Trade-offs**: Sweeping fighters do not carry ordnance to damage runways; risk heavy casualties against dense enemy flak if pulled into low altitude.

### Pattern: Pilot Fatigue & Training Pipeline Cycling
- **When to use**: Sustaining air arm operational effectiveness across multi-month campaigns without depleting ace cadres.
- **How**:
  1. Withdraw frontline squadrons when average pilot fatigue exceeds 30 or morale drops below 60.
  2. Transfer high-hour veterans (EXP > 75) into training commands or reserve pools to mentor rookie pools.
  3. Assign green replacement pilots (EXP 40–50) to rear-area ASW or rear transport duty until basic navigation and flight skills reach 60+.
- **Trade-offs**: Slows frontline reinforcement flow; requires administrative attention to air group roster management.

## 3. Ground Combat Patterns

### Pattern: Deliberate vs. Shock Attack Progression
- **When to use**: Executing land offensive operations against enemy fortified base perimeters.
- **How**:
  1. *Reconnaissance & Artillery Preparation*: Conduct aerial photo-reconnaissance to ascertain exact enemy Assault Value (AV) and Fort level. Initiate daily artillery bombardment attacks to disrupt defender cohesion without taking assault casualties.
  2. *Deliberate Attack (Phase 1)*: Launch Deliberate Attacks when ratio is projected at ≥ 2:1. Deliberate attacks test defenses, degrade enemy fortifications by 1 level, and preserve attacker cohesion.
  3. *Shock Attack (Phase 2)*: Switch to Shock Attack ONLY after enemy forts have been reduced to 0 or 1, and defender disruption/fatigue is elevated. Shock attacks double raw attack power to trigger retreats.
- **Trade-offs**: Shock attacks against intact forts (Fort 3+) cause catastrophic attacker casualties, disorganization, and severe counter-attacks.

### Pattern: Amphibious Assault Preparation & Landing
- **When to use**: Seizing island atolls or enemy-held coastal positions.
- **How**:
  1. *Preparation Level*: Ensure landing divisions accumulate 100 Preparation Points assigned specifically to the target base hex (takes several weeks in rear staging bases).
  2. *Naval & Air Softening*: Execute heavy warship naval bombardments (BB/CA) and continuous carrier airstrikes targeting shore batteries and airfield complexes for 2–3 turns prior to landing.
  3. *Specialized Craft*: Utilize Attack Transports (APA), Attack Cargo Ships (AKA), and Landing Craft (LST, LCI) rather than conventional merchant cargo ships to accelerate unloading and minimize offloading casualties.
  4. *Base Force & Aviation Support*: Land follow-up Base Forces within 24 hours of beachhead capture to establish immediate operational CAP and air defense.
- **Trade-offs**: Diverts fleet capital assets to stationary bombardment tracks vulnerable to submarine ambush.

## 4. Production & Economy Patterns (Japanese Industrial Machine)

### Pattern: The Resource-to-Heavy-Industry Balance
- **When to use**: Managing the Japanese home island war production economy.
- **How**:
  1. *Balanced Ratio Tracking*:
     - 1 Heavy Industry (HI) center consumes: 2 Resources + 1 Oil/Fuel → Yields: 1 Supply + 1 Fuel/Supply balance point + points for R&D/Production.
  2. *Raw Material Conveyor*: Maintain dedicated merchant shipping corridors moving Crude Oil from DEI (Palembang, Balikpapan) to Home Island refineries, and Resources from Malaya/Manchuria to Honshu factories.
  3. *Refinery Optimization*: Avoid over-expanding non-essential shipyards or aircraft factories that outstrip daily resource supply receipts.
- **Trade-offs**: Over-prioritizing merchant building sacrifices escort warship production needed to defend the trade routes.

### Pattern: Targeted Aircraft Engine & Airframe R&D Acceleration
- **When to use**: Accelerating next-generation IJN/IJA fighter and bomber models to overcome Allied qualitative dominance.
- **How**:
  1. Standardize aircraft production around standard engine families (e.g., Ha-33, Ha-45, Nakajima Homare).
  2. Allocate research factories to key models (e.g., A7M Reppu, Ki-84 Frank) months in advance.
  3. Ensure repair/conversion points are maintained to swap factory lines without halting active engine flows.
- **Trade-offs**: Premature factory conversions consume massive quantities of supplies and idle aircraft production for several months.
