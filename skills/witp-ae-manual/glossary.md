# War in the Pacific: Admiral's Edition (WitP-AE) - Master Glossary

## A
- **A6M Zero (Type 0 Carrier Fighter)** — Premier Japanese carrier-borne fighter aircraft featuring exceptional maneuverability and range but lacking armor and self-sealing fuel tanks in early models (Ch 07).
- **AAR (After Action Report)** — Combat summary log generated after movement and combat execution phases detailing losses, hits, and operational results (Ch 05).
- **Admin Move (Administrative Movement)** — Strategic ground movement mode along railway/road networks requiring friendly hex control and allowing faster transit with zero combat capability (Ch 08).
- **Air Balance (CAP / Sweep / Strike)** — Ratio setting of aircraft squadrons allocated between Combat Air Patrol, fighter sweeps, and offensive escort/bombing (Ch 07).
- **Air Base Size (Level 1–9)** — Portrays runway length, parking infrastructure, and air operational capacity; determines maximum supportable aircraft without penalty (Ch 09).
- **Air Combat Resolution** — Phase-driven dogfight and flak sequence calculating detection, altitude advantage, maneuver rating, gunnery, and defensive fire (Ch 07, 19).
- **Air Support** — Aviation support ground devices (mechanics, armories, fueling crews) required at an airbase (1 point per active engine) to avoid maintenance penalties (Ch 07, 09).
- **Altitude Advantage** — Tactical combat modifier in air-to-air combat; aircraft diving from higher altitude gain initial shot bonuses and speed increases (Ch 07, 19).
- **Amphibious Assault (Amphib TF)** — Task force mission designed for landing troops onto enemy-held or contested beachheads; faces severe defensive fire and unloading delays without prep (Ch 06, 08).
- **Anti-Air (AA) Value** — Composite score measuring ship or ground unit effectiveness against attacking enemy aircraft (Ch 06, 08).
- **Anti-Submarine Warfare (ASW)** — Specialized combat mission and capability using sonar, depth charges, hedgehogs, and patrol aircraft to detect and destroy enemy submarines (Ch 06).
- **Armament Factory** — Production facility consuming resources and heavy industry output to fabricate ground ordnance, artillery, and vehicle replacements (Ch 13).
- **Assault Value (AV)** — Measure of a ground unit's frontline combat strength used in attack and defense odds calculations (Ch 08).
- **Auto-Sub / Auto-Convoy** — AI routine settings permitting automated convoy dispatch, escort assignment, and patrol routing (Ch 06, 15).
- **Aviation Support** — Ground support squads dedicated to maintaining and arming aircraft engines (Ch 08, 09).

## B
- **B-17 / B-24 / Heavy Bombers** — Long-range strategic multi-engine bombers utilized for industrial bombardment, port raids, and naval surface interdiction (Ch 07).
- **Base Force** — Specialized naval/military ground formation providing aviation support, engineers, coastal artillery, AA defenses, and local headquarters support (Ch 08, 09).
- **Bombardment (Naval/Ground)** — High-explosive shell saturation targeting ports, airfields, ground troops, and supply dumps (Ch 06, 08).
- **Bonus Turns / Surprise Round** — Opening turn mechanics (December 7, 1941) affording the Japanese player enhanced movement, air surprise, and torpedo efficacy (Ch 02, 18).

## C
- **CAP (Combat Air Patrol)** — Defensive fighter assignment protecting the home airfield, task force, or neighboring hexes against incoming aerial strikes (Ch 07).
- **Card-Sort / Filter Displays** — In-game interface tools allowing sorting of ships, air units, leaders, and task forces by range, readiness, or status (Ch 04, 05).
- **Carrier Division (CarDiv)** — Operational pairing or group of aircraft carriers operating coordinated air wings and common tactical doctrines (Ch 06, 07).
- **Coastal Defense Guns (CD)** — Fortified heavy artillery emplaced at base hexes capable of engaging approaching enemy naval task forces (Ch 08, 09).
- **Combat Readiness / Disruption** — Measure of cohesion degradation suffered by ground units or air crews following combat, bombardment, or forced marching (Ch 07, 08).
- **Command Boundary / Political Restriction** — Red/Green bordered unit restrictions requiring political points to cross geographic theater commands (e.g. Kwantung Army) (Ch 11, 18).
- **Conversion (Ship)** — Shipyard shipyard overhaul changing a vessel from one class or mission role to another (e.g., liner to armed merchant cruiser) (Ch 06, 14).
- **Critical Hit (Naval)** — Penetrating damage hit bypassing standard armor to detonate magazines, disable boilers, or flood machine spaces (Ch 06, 19).

## D
- **Damage Control (DC)** — Naval ship rating and procedure determining how fast onboard crews contain fires and shore up progressive flooding (Ch 06, 14).
- **Day / Night Surface Combat** — Surface engagements occurring under varying visibility and optical/radar search conditions (Ch 06, 19).
- **Depot Hex** — Logistics distribution hub configured with high supply priorities to stockpile provisions for forward echelons (Ch 09, 15).
- **Detection Level (DL 0–10)** — Numerical tracking index of an enemy unit's location accuracy; directly controls airstrike targeting and interception odds (Ch 10).
- **Disruption** — Temporary loss of unit fighting effectiveness; recovers daily if supply and fatigue levels are favorable (Ch 08).
- **Drop Tanks** — Auxiliary jettisonable fuel tanks providing fighter aircraft extended escort range at the cost of slight initial dogfight penalties (Ch 07).
- **Dry Dock** — Specialized port shipyard infrastructure required to repair heavy flotation and major system damage on large warships (Ch 09, 14).
- **Drop / Transfer Plane** — Interface commands reallocating aircraft airframes between squadrons, depots, and pilot training reserves (Ch 07).

## E
- **Economy of Scale (Industry)** — Japanese manufacturing mechanics whereby matching resource intake, crude oil, and refinery outputs maintains maximum supply generation (Ch 13).
- **Engine R&D / Airframe R&D** — Japanese industrial research tracks accelerating production availability dates for advanced late-war aircraft models (Ch 13).
- **Escort (TF / Air)** — Protective units assigned to shield vulnerable transports, tankers, or bomber strike packages (Ch 06, 07).
- **Evacuation (Troop / Base)** — Loading and withdrawing military personnel from endangered perimeter outposts (Ch 06, 08).
- **Experience (EXP 0–99)** — Numerical measure of unit or pilot skill; affects gunnery accuracy, dogfight survival, bomb hits, and leader initiative (Ch 07, 08).

## F
- **Fast Transport TF (Tokyo Express)** — High-speed destroyer/cruiser task force dedicated to night troop/supply delivery to contested hexes with minimal port facilities (Ch 06).
- **Fatigue (FAT 0–99)** — Accumulation of physical exhaustion in pilots, ground troops, and naval crews degrading combat capability (Ch 06, 07, 08).
- **Flak (Anti-Aircraft Fire)** — Ground and shipboard cannon/machinegun fire directed at attacking aircraft (Ch 06, 07, 08).
- **Flotation Damage (FLT)** — Hull hull breach measurement (0–100); reaching 100 results in vessel sinking (Ch 06, 14).
- **Fortifications (Forts 1–9)** — Defensive base structures reducing incoming ground and air assault casualties and multiplying defender Assault Value (Ch 08, 09).
- **Fuel (Refined Petroleum)** — Logistics commodity consumed by naval task forces and heavy industry (Ch 06, 13, 15).

## G
- **General Headquarters (GHQ)** — Top-level theater command structure providing supply tracing, reinforcement channeling, and coordination bonuses (Ch 08, 11).
- **Ground Combat Modes** — Unit operational postures: Combat, Defend, Move, Reserve, and Rest (Ch 08).
- **Ground Support Points** — Maintenance units servicing vehicles, field artillery, and infantry weapons (Ch 08).

## H
- **Heavy Industry (HI)** — Japanese economic engine units consuming coal and iron ore to produce supplies, political points, and fuel (Ch 13).
- **Hex (40 Nautical Miles)** — Fundamental geographic map grid unit in WitP-AE representing distance, movement cost, and zone of control (Ch 04).
- **Hold Order** — Administrative instruction preventing units from automatically drawing replacement squads, airframes, or expanding bases (Ch 07, 08, 09).

## I
- **Industrial Center** — Geographic urban base maintaining factories for aircraft engines, vehicle assembly, ship building, or synthetic fuel (Ch 13).
- **Invasion Hex** — Coastal base or non-base land hex designated as target for amphibious task force disembarkation (Ch 06, 08).
- **Island Capacity (Base Stacking)** — Maximum ground and aviation troop limit an atoll or island can support before suffering extreme attrition (Ch 09, 20).

## K
- **Kido Butai (First Air Fleet)** — Imperial Japanese Navy carrier battle fleet comprising 6 fleet carriers at the start of WWII (Ch 06, 07).
- **Kwantung Army** — Japanese military garrison in Manchukuo bound by political point transfer restrictions to prevent premature redeployment (Ch 11, 18).

## L
- **Leader Ratings (Inspir, Admin, Land, Air, Sea)** — Numerical attributes governing leader initiative, retreat survival, fatigue recovery, and strike coordination (Ch 05, 06, 07, 08).
- **Logistics Pipeline** — Movement network of cargo freighters (AK), tankers (TK), rail, and roads moving supplies and fuel from origins to combat theaters (Ch 15).

## M
- **Major Damage / Minor Damage** — Naval damage distinction; minor repaired pierside or by repair ships; major requires drydocks or shipyard time (Ch 14).
- **Manpower Centers** — Population bases generating raw recruits for ground unit replenishment and industrial workforces (Ch 13, 16).
- **Minefield (Defensive / Offensive)** — Hex hazard deployed by minelayers or submarines targeting enemy naval traffic entering the hex (Ch 06).
- **Missions (Air)** — Squadron assignments: CAP, Sweep, Escort, Nav Search, ASW, Airfield Attack, Port Attack, Naval Attack, Ground Attack, Transport (Ch 07).
- **Missions (Naval)** — TF assignments: Surface Combat, Air Combat, Bombardment, Fast Transport, Amphibious, Transport, Cargo, Tanker, Minelaying, Minesweeping, Submarine (Ch 06).

## N
- **Naval Search** — Routine aerial reconnaissance grid flown to identify and track enemy task forces (Ch 07, 10).
- **Night Operations (Air / Sea)** — High-risk night surface gunfire actions and night naval airstrikes requiring high experience and radar (Ch 06, 07, 19).

## O
- **Oil Centers / Crude Oil** — Raw natural resource pumped in Sumatra, Borneo, and Burma required to feed oil refineries (Ch 13, 15).
- **Operational Loss (Ops Loss)** — Non-combat loss of aircraft during takeoff, landing, adverse weather, or mechanical failure (Ch 07).
- **Overstacking Penalty** — Severe attrition, combat, and servicing penalties imposed when aircraft or ground units exceed base or hex capacity limits (Ch 07, 08, 09).

## P
- **PBEM (Play By Email)** — Turn-based multiplayer mode utilizing encrypted turn files and anti-tamper security routines (Ch 02, 20).
- **Perimeter Defense** — Japanese strategic strategy anchoring Pacific defense along key island fortress chains (Ch 01, 17).
- **Pier-Side Repair** — Ship maintenance performed without drydocking for system damage not affecting structural float integrity (Ch 14).
- **Pilot Fatigue** — Accumulation of flight hours degrading gunnery, bombing accuracy, and survivability (Ch 07).
- **Pilot Training (TRAIN / RESERVE)** — Pilot replacement system allowing off-map training of rookie aviators before front-line deployment (Ch 07).
- **Political Points (PP)** — Resource spent to buy out unit restrictions, replace commanders, and reassign divisions to active commands (Ch 11).
- **Port Size (Level 1–9)** — Port capacity dictating docking limit, cargo unloading speed, and vessel repair infrastructure (Ch 09, 14, 15).
- **Preparation Points (Prep 0–100)** — Ground and air unit planning score allocated to a specific target base; enhances combat performance and reduces landing casualties (Ch 08).

## R
- **Radar (Naval / Air / Land)** — Early electronic detection devices improving target acquisition, gunnery accuracy, and night interception (Ch 06, 07, 19).
- **Radius of Action (Max / Normal Range)** — Aerial operational combat radius determining escort cover, strike reach, and ferry distances (Ch 07).
- **Rail Movement** — High-speed land strategic transit across connected friendly rail hexes (Ch 04, 08).
- **Reconnaissance (Air Recon)** — High-altitude photographic flights uncovering enemy troop strengths, fort levels, and base stockpiles (Ch 07, 10).
- **Refinery (Fuel Production)** — Industrial installations converting raw crude oil into usable heavy ship fuel and industrial supplies (Ch 13).
- **Reinforcements** — Scheduled arrival of warships, merchantmen, aircraft airframes, and army divisions based on historical timetables (Ch 16).
- **Repair Ship (AR / AE / AD / AS)** — Auxiliary ships enabling advanced forward-base repairs, torpedo reload, and destroyer maintenance without full dockyards (Ch 06, 14).
- **Replacements** — Individual infantry squads, artillery tubes, and pilots drawn from manpower pools to rebuild depleted active formations (Ch 16).
- **Resources (Coal / Iron)** — Basic raw materials gathered to feed Heavy Industry centers (Ch 13).
- **Rest & Refit** — Unit posture dedicated to reducing fatigue and disruption and assimilating new draft replacements (Ch 07, 08).
- **Routing Waypoints** — Intermediate navigation plot points configured in Task Force screens to bypass submarine choke points or enemy air zones (Ch 06).

## S
- **Second Wave (Carrier Air Strike)** — Follow-up carrier air package organized if flight decks and ordnance handling remain unimpeded (Ch 07).
- **Shock Attack** — Aggressive all-out ground assault posture doubling attacking assault value but incurring heavy attacker disruption and losses if repulsed (Ch 08).
- **Strait Control** — Maritime choke points (e.g., Malacca, Sunda, Tsugaru) where coastal forts or land hex ownership restrict naval passage (Ch 06, 20).
- **Submarine Doctrine** — Rules governing fleet boat deployment, unrestricted warfare constraints, and early torpedo dud rates (Ch 06, 18).
- **Supply Pipeline / Required Supply** — Base consumption demands needed for civilian survival, base operations, fortification construction, and combat fire (Ch 09, 15).
- **Surface Combat (SCTF)** — Heavy warship squadron designed for surface gunnery, torpedo broadsides, and night fleet engagements (Ch 06, 19).
- **Surprise Factor** — Tactical combat modifier granting opening unreturned salvos in naval ambushes or surprise air raids (Ch 06, 07, 19).
- **Sweep Mission** — Dedicated fighter offensive patrol over an enemy base aimed at clearing defending CAP before strike bombers arrive (Ch 07).

## T
- **Task Force (TF)** — Naval formation grouping ships under a single commander and mission profile (Ch 06).
- **Task Force Speed (Cruise / Full / Mission)** — Operational speed settings dictating endurance, fuel consumption, and detection vulnerability (Ch 06).
- **Time on Target (TOT)** — Coordinated air strikes timed to saturate enemy defenses concurrently (Ch 07).
- **Torpedo Dud Rates** — Historical failure simulation rendering US Mark 14 torpedoes highly prone to premature detonation, deep running, or contact failure through 1943 (Ch 06, 18).
- **Transport Task Force** — Merchant shipping formation dedicated to ferrying troops, equipment, and heavy weapons (Ch 06, 15).

## U
- **Unrestricted Submarine Warfare** — Rules of engagement lifting historical target verification constraints for Allied submarines (Ch 18).
- **Upgrade (Aircraft / Ship)** — Replacing obsolete aircraft models or warship AA/radar systems with newer technological marks (Ch 07, 14).

## V
- **Victory Points (VP)** — Quantitative scoring metrics awarded for sinking ships, destroying aircraft, taking enemy bases, and strategic bombing (Ch 17).

## W
- **Waypoint Navigation** — Setting multi-leg patrol and transit paths for naval task forces to dodge hostile reconnaissance umbrellas (Ch 06).
- **Weather Zone** — Dynamic climatic areas dictating cloud cover, squalls, monsoons, and sea states that hinder aviation and movement (Ch 12).
- **Withdrawal Date** — Scheduled mandatory removal of specific Allied ships, aircraft squadrons, and ground units to European/SEAC theaters on penalty of severe VP deductions (Ch 16, 17).
