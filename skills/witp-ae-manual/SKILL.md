## Core Operational Frameworks & Mental Models

### 1. The Naval Operations & Task Force Doctrine (Chapter 6 & 19)
- **Speed Homogeneity**: TF cruising speed and movement points are dictated by the slowest vessel in the task group. Never combine 12-knot replenishment auxiliaries with 28-knot fleet combatants unless in protected rear convoys.
- **Escort Density Rule**: Maintain a screening ratio of at least 1.5 to 2.0 destroyers/escorts per capital ship (CV, BB, CA) or major merchant hull (AK, AP). Lower ratios sharply increase submarine torpedo penetration rates.
- **Damage Control Dynamics**: Ships suffer three distinct damage vectors:
  - *Flotation (FLT)*: Breaches hull integrity. FLT ≥ 50 demands immediate reduction to Cruise Speed (5–8 knots) and routing to Port Level ≥ 3. FLT = 100 sinks the ship.
  - *System (SYS)*: Knocks out electronics, radar, and fire control. Repairable pierside with repair ships (AR) or in standard ports.
  - *Fire (FIRE)*: Directly risks ammunition explosions. Immediate onboard DC focus takes priority over maneuvering.
- **Surface Combat Leadership**: Assign surface battle line commanders with high Naval (SEA) and Aggressiveness ratings to secure opening salvo advantages and accurate radar/optical broadsides.

### 2. Carrier & Land-Based Air Doctrine (Chapter 7 & 19)
- **Layered CAP Umbrella**:
  - *High Altitude (25,000–30,000 ft)*: Intercepts enemy fighter sweeps and secures diving kinetic advantage.
  - *Medium Altitude (15,000–20,000 ft)*: Engages incoming level bombers and primary strike escorts.
  - *Low Altitude (5,000–10,000 ft)*: Intercepts low-altitude torpedo bombers approaching naval formations.
- **Aviation Support Ratio**: Exactly 1 Aviation Support Point per active aircraft engine is required. Base support deficits directly reduce daily sortie rates and spike maintenance-induced aborts.
- **Pilot Fatigue Governance**: Rotate squadrons to Rest or Reserve whenever pilot fatigue reaches 30. High fatigue (> 30) exponentially increases operational takeoff/landing accidents and slashes dogfight survivability.

### 3. Ground Assault & Amphibious Doctrine (Chapter 8 & 9)
- **Preparation Points (Prep 100)**: Units accumulating 100 prep points for a specific base gain maximum combat survivability, reduced disruption, and full assault value. Landing without preparation multiplies beachhead casualties by up to 3×.
- **Deliberate vs. Shock Attack**:
  - *Deliberate Attack*: Standard siege posture. Preserves unit cohesion, reduces enemy fort levels by 1 on a 2:1 odds roll, and minimizes attacker disorganization.
  - *Shock Attack*: All-out assault doubling attack value to force enemy retreats. Use ONLY after fortifications are reduced to 0–1 and enemy units are disorganized.
- **Ground Support Balance**: 1 support squad per 10 combat/artillery squads prevents massive march disruption.

### 4. Japanese Industrial War Economy (Chapter 13 & 15)
- **The Industrial Conversion Equation**:
  - `1 Heavy Industry (HI)` consumes `2 Resources + 1 Crude Oil/Fuel` → Produces `1 Supply + 1 Fuel Balance + Production Points`.
  - Disruption of the sea corridor between the Southern Resources Area (DEI) and the Home Islands directly strangles aircraft engine fabrication and fleet fuel reserves.
- **R&D and Engine Standardization**: Concentrate factory conversions on standard engine families (Nakajima Homare, Mitsubishi Ha-33/Ha-45) to avoid fragmented, idle assembly lines.
